# Strategic Discovery Map (Module 1)

## Responses
- **Write one feature from the map that, if an engineer asked “why is this a priority for our 12-week roadmap?”, you honestly could not give a strategic answer for.:** The one I couldn't defend is the weather and activity adjustment under Goals.

I put it on the map because it's a common feature in competing apps and sounds smart. That is a reason to list it in a brainstorm, but it isn't a reason to spend part of 12 weeks on it. If an engineer asked why it's a priority, I'd have nothing beyond "other apps do it."
- **Write one feature that looks “correct” for the app but has zero strategic weight, you have no idea if it helps the company make money or whether a single user actually asked for it.:** The one I'd pick is the badges half of "Streaks, badges."

Badges look right for this kind of app. Nearly every habit tracker has them, they're easy to explain, and they make a roadmap look like it has engagement covered. But I can't connect them to anything that matters:

Users: I have no evidence that anyone has asked for badges. My only basis is that other apps have them, and that is an assumption about the category, not something we've heard from our users.
Money: I can't say whether badges lift retention enough to affect revenue, or whether they would ever feed a paid tier. Nobody pays for a "7 days in a row" icon, and I can't point to a metric they would move.
Cost: They aren't free. Someone has to design the art, define the unlock rules, write the copy, and maintain it all as the app changes.
- **Compare V3 to your Step 1 baseline. What changed, and is the output objectively better, did the AI get smarter, or did your added PM knowledge (JTBD, effort/impact, retention) force it to be useful?:** The AI didn't get smarter but the prompt did. 
For a discovery sprint, yes. A team could run a meeting from it, and they couldn't from the first map. But "objectively better" overstates it, because they answer different requests. The first map did what it was asked to do.
- **Key takeaway: how does this prove the PM’s role is to think while the AI’s role is to execute?:** I'd put the claim this way: the PM owns the question, the evidence, and the decision, and the AI is a fast drafter and sparring partner whose output has to be checked.
