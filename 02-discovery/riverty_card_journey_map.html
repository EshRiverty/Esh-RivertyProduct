Future-state journey: a Riverty card for multi-provider pay-later users

Persona: salaried Germans aged 25 to 40 who use BNPL on most non-trivial purchases, hold two or more pay-later providers, and sometimes want to pay later in a store. P6 (31, Stuttgart) embodies both halves; P4 covers the in-store gap.

Goal: buy what they want, online or in a store, and have money leave their account only for what they decide to keep, without tracking it by hand.

Concept stage. The research data is synthetic, and the internal states are inferred, not measured.

Four-stage future-state journey

Card in wallet

Adds virtual card to phone wallet → leading to ready for stores and online at once.
Wary of a fourth card → leading to won over by one place for pay-later.
Wallet crowding and low brand trust → leading to a reason to carry Riverty.
2

Pay anywhere

Pay later at tills

Taps card at the till, chooses pay later → leading to deferral works outside partner shops.
Relieved → leading to pay-later feels the same in stores as online.
No pay-later at tills → leading to no forced debit fallback.
3

Track

One view of dues

Opens one view of card purchases and due dates → leading to no manual calendar entries.
Calm → leading to trusts one reminder source instead of double-checking her calendar.
Scattered due dates and near-misses → leading to fewer surprises on card purchases.
4

Settle

Pay after deciding

Keeps or returns items within the window → leading to pays only for what she keeps.
In control → leading to money moves after her decision, not before.
Money leaving before the keep-or-return decision → leading to timing follows her choice.

Three advantages over the manual workaround

  1. One view replaces calendar entries → leading to no manual upkeep for card purchases, fewer missed dates.
  2. Pay-later at any till → leading to deferral survives where the manual workaround falls back to debit.
  3. Payment timing follows the keep-or-return decision → leading to money leaves only for what she keeps.

Limits of this map

  • Stage 3 covers card purchases only. Klarna and PayPal due dates stay outside the view unless most spend moves to the card.
  • Stage 4 is untested. Payment timing that follows return windows needs a risk and regulatory check, and consumer credit rules from 20 November 2026 may limit it.
  • Stage 1 is the weakest. Wallet crowding was the biggest barrier in the research, and nothing here shows the card overcomes it.
