# Hypothesis & Success Metrics (Module 3)

## Pre-work · Hypothesis check
- **Role , who you are solving for (from M2):** Heavy German Riverty users who shop mostly online and use pay-later mainly to receive the goods before paying. The deck's target is the roughly 82k app users with five or more purchases in 2022 (an approximation), out of about 511k heavy and hardcore users overall. In the interviews they typically hold PayPal and Klarna alongside Riverty, and several used Riverty only because it was the sole option offered. The brief's Lena fits this profile, but the interviews don't confirm her age or situation.
- **Goal , what this user is ultimately trying to achieve:** To pay only for what they keep, and to stay in control of what they owe. The interviews give this as returns and security: goods arrive and the money isn't gone, with no back-and-forth payments. Several also want an overview of their spending, such as a budget book or categories, and some worry about overspending or forgetting a payment.
- **Friction / moment of misery , the specific pain blocking their goal:** Payment timing and tracking don't follow what they've decided. The evidence points to three connected pains:

Open returns. Klarna freezes the payment when a return is reported, and participants wanted that added to the Riverty concept. Return processes were called intransparent elsewhere.
Losing track. One participant had forgotten a payment before, another dislikes invoice-only purchases because of this risk, and one wants the app to remember due dates.
Forced first contact. One participant had a terrible experience: they knew nothing about Riverty, had to set up an account, and the payment was deferred when they wanted to pay directly.
- **Current workaround , the external tool or manual process they rely on (M2):** The research documents several workarounds, but no single one dominates:

Budget books and budget apps, such as Finanzguru and Monkey, to see and categorize spending.
Klarna's app for payment reminders.
Paying invoices within two or three days, which gives up the deferral to avoid forgetting.
Withdrawing cash at the start of the month and paying everything from it.
Payback and similar cashback apps, which one participant said they sometimes forget to use.
- **Problem Hook , your one-sentence framing of the business crisis (M1):** Customers like Lena juggles BNPL apps and pays in stores with someone else's card, and with only 6% brand preference, each month Riverty waits to launch a card lets Klarna and PayPal tighten their hold on her and leaves Riverty a replaceable checkout button.
- **Value Proposition , the outcome your initiative promised to deliver (M1):** Value proposition: For busy BNPL users like Lena, who want money to leave their account only after they've decided to keep what they bought, the Riverty card lets them pay later anywhere, in stores and online, with one clear view of what's due. Unlike Klarna's debit-first card or PayPal's separate instalment products, it keeps the "decide first, pay after" habit in every setting and sends users back to Riverty's partner merchants instead of away from them.
New value: Riverty moves from a checkout option Lena doesn't prefer to an everyday card she carries. That closes the in-store and heavy-user gaps from the brief and gives Riverty a direct consumer relationship for the first time.

Why now:

Klarna's card base is growing, and PayPal now offers instalments at Amazon.de and a virtual card for in-store payments, so Lena's habits are being set by competitors. From 20 November 2026, most BNPL falls under consumer credit rules, and Riverty carries that compliance cost with or without a card. Riverty's banking licence and Mastercard platform are already in place, and a consumer card is the next product that can earn that investment.

## Step 3 · Craft your hypothesis
- **Qualitative evidence (from M2) , quote the specific friction / moment of misery for your persona:** "Payment should be paused until return was confirmed." Another participant called the same rule "Very important (reason to use BNPL in first place)." The friction is that money can leave before the user has decided what to keep.
- **Quantitative evidence (from M3) , name the metric or data point that confirms the pain; cite the number:** 70.5% of Riverty users in Germany (30.4% strongly, 40.1% somewhat) agree that managing multiple payment systems makes it harder to keep track of their spending (Norstat survey, n=1,022).
- **Persona , role, goal, and the friction you confirmed in the reconciliation steps:** Role: a heavy German online shopper and existing Riverty user (five or more purchases a year, around 82k app users in the deck) who also holds PayPal or Klarna.
Goal: pay only for what they keep, and stay in control of what they owe.
Confirmed friction: payment timing doesn't follow the return decision, with Klarna's pause as the benchmark.
- **Problem you are solving , one sentence describing the specific friction this initiative removes:** The card makes payment pause and the invoice adjust as soon as a return is registered, on any purchase made with it, not only at partner merchants.
- **Strategic outcome , what behaviour change do you expect, and how does it map to retention / revenue / churn?:** I expect heavy users to choose the card over whichever BNPL option is offered, and to use it for more purchases.
Retention: more frequent use among the roughly 511k heavy and hardcore users, which the deck frames as a strategic KPI.
Revenue: weak per transaction. Replacing the 1.54% merchant fee with 0.1% interchange takes contribution margin from 1.3% to −0.1%, so revenue depends on extra volume, Flex conversion and adjacent services.
Churn: the biggest leak is one-time users (1.64 million of 3.16 million in 2022 used Riverty once), and this card doesn't address that.
- **Primary success metric (initiative signal) , the leading indicator that tells you the gap is closing:** The share of activated cardholders who make a second card purchase within 30 days of their first. It shows whether the card is becoming the habit that Riverty's strategic KPIs depend on, and it's measurable within weeks. A companion measure is the share of card returns where the invoice pauses or adjusts without a support contact, since that is the friction the card is meant to remove. As a starting target, I'd propose that at least half of activated cardholders make a second purchase within 30 days.
- **Guardrail metric (product signal) , the metric that must NOT drop; it protects your existing base:** Integrated checkout volume per heavy user at partner merchants. If users switch the same purchases onto the card, Riverty swaps a 1.54% merchant fee for 0.1% interchange, and contribution margin falls from 1.3% to −0.1% on that volume, per the deck's ball-park figures. This also protects the merchant-first positioning.
- **Decision window , how much time or data before you scale, pivot, or kill? minimum threshold to proceed?:** Scale if activation reaches the brief's modeled 70% or higher, the repeat-use target above is met, and the guardrail holds.
Pivot if activation is fine but repeat use is weak. That would mean users like the card but it isn't winning the everyday purchase.
Kill if activation falls below about 50%, or if the volume cannibalized from integrated checkout costs more margin than the card earns.
- **Draft your full hypothesis sentence , one to three sentences; quote the metric, name the persona, name the outcome:** If heavy German Riverty users who also hold PayPal or Klarna can use one Riverty card to pay later at any online shop, with all their card spending categorized in a single overview, then at least 50% of activated cardholders will make a second card purchase within 30 days, because today they use Riverty only where a merchant happens to offer it, and the card makes it available wherever they already shop. We will know we're wrong if activation falls below about 50% or if integrated checkout volume per heavy user at partner merchants drops by more than about 5% against a matched group.
